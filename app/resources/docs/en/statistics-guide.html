SAI Statistics — Statistics Guide

A quick decision chain for "which test should I use?". It is not statistical advice — it maps common questions to the matching menu path. For complex designs (several factors, mixed models, repeated measures with covariates, etc.) consult a statistician.

Step 0 — Check normality first. If your dependent variable is continuous (scale), open Normality View (Ctrl+4) before choosing between parametric and nonparametric tests. It evaluates the variable automatically with Shapiro-Wilk, Lilliefors (corrected K-S) and a Q-Q plot. With small samples (n < 50) rely more on Shapiro-Wilk; with large samples rely more on the plots (Q-Q, histogram).

1-Sample K-S... (Nonparametric Tests) checks whether the data follow a given distribution — it does not test whether the median equals a value. To check normality, prefer Normality View (Lilliefors and Shapiro-Wilk).
Not sure? Every dialog's Paste R button shows exactly which R function your settings will call — a quick way to verify what the test actually does.

Small Samples or Tied Values

For nonparametric tests SAI reports an exact p-value whenever possible (through R's own default or the coin package) — even where SPSS Base offers only an asymptotic one. The result table shows both Asymp. Sig. (for comparison with SPSS) and Exact Sig. (the more accurate value). With small or sparse samples, rely on Exact Sig.
